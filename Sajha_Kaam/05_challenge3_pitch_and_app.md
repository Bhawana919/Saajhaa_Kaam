# Challenge 3 pitch: fixing the "cousin" problem

**Track:** 3️⃣ Global Clients, Investors & Diaspora. *"Build the platform that bridges Nepali talent with the world."*

## The problem with the current story

In the Anjali/Ramesh story, Ramesh finds Anjali because **his cousin says** "Anjali in Surkhet is a good designer." That is hiring through networks, which is exactly what Challenge 2 says is broken ("Nepal's hiring system runs on networks, not merit"). A judge can fairly ask: *"So your bridge only works if you already have a cousin?"*

The evidence backs that worry. In a J-PAL field experiment, when men were asked to refer candidates, only **30% of referrals were women**. Referral hiring makes it harder for groups that start out disadvantaged.¹ For a rural woman in Surkhet with no relatives abroad, a network-only bridge doesn't reach her.

## The reframe (one sentence for the judges)

> **"Networks are welcome as a door, never as the filter. Sajha Kaam turns one cousin's word into proof any stranger can verify, and gives people with no cousin a way in."**

| Stage | Before | With Sajha Kaam |
|---|---|---|
| Job #1 | Only if a relative vouches for you | **Two doors:** a relative shares your link, **or** you apply on the **Pahilo Kaam** board (first-timers only) and a client picks you in **Blind merit mode** |
| Choosing someone | "My cousin knows her" | Name, photo, gender and district hidden until shortlist; judged on samples and client-confirmed jobs; **1 in 3 profiles shown is new talent** |
| Job #2 onwards | Back to asking relatives | The **Work Passport QR** is client-confirmed and tamper-evident, so a stranger in London trusts it without knowing anyone |

So the cousin is a starting point. Sajha Kaam makes sure the second job doesn't need one.

## Why this is Challenge 3, not Challenge 2

- The buyer is the **diaspora and global client**. The product's job is to let Ramesh in Sydney and a café in London buy from Nepal safely.
- **Diaspora capital = diaspora purchasing power.** We don't run an investment product (no securities, no pooled money). Instead, the **Impact tab** shows each diaspora business how much it has paid into Nepal, how many first-ever jobs it created and which provinces it reached. The **Pahilo Kaam Pledge** asks them to give one small paid job a month to someone with no track record.
- It runs in both directions: Nepali talent reaches the world through proof, and the world reaches Nepali talent without needing personal contacts.

## Updated story (use this in the pitch and video)

1. Ramesh (Sydney) types in Nepali: *"मेरो पसलको दसैं अफरका लागि ५ वटा फेसबुक पोस्ट चाहियो"*.
2. The AI drafts the Kaam Card **and flags 5 things he never said** (size, logo, language, price, deadline), the exact gaps that caused the old fight.
3. His cousin had mentioned Anjali. **Even without that**, Ramesh could open Find Talent, see "Talent #860 · new talent · café menu poster, Teej greeting", shortlist her by her samples and only then see her name.
4. Anjali accepts. She can have the card read aloud in Nepali. She delivers in 4 days.
5. Ramesh can only confirm after ticking every "done when" item. He pays her Khalti directly. **Only Anjali can mark the payment received.**
6. Her Passport shows: *5 Dashain offer posts · Ramesh K., Sydney · Client-confirmed · Payment received · SK-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx*.
7. A London shop owner scans her QR. Every proof code is re-checked on the spot. She gets hired by **a stranger**, and that is the bridge.

## Unique features to name on stage

1. **"Things you didn't say"**: AI clarity flags that stop disputes before work starts.
2. **Bilingual Kaam Card with Nepali read-aloud**: both sides read the same terms in their own language.
3. **Fair-pay guard**: shows the usual price range and warns when an offer is below it.
4. **Checklist-gated confirmation + two-sided payment confirmation**: "done" and "paid" are facts, not opinions.
5. **Tamper-evident Work Passport**: each entry has a proof code; the public page shows "✗ record was edited" if anyone changes it.
6. **Blind merit mode + fair rotation**: hiring by work, not by who you know.
7. **Pahilo Kaam board + Pledge**: a first job for people with no network.
8. **Safety by default**: no phone number shared, photo hidden, province instead of district, report/block on every card.
9. **No custody of money**: payment goes straight to Khalti. Khalti now lets freelancers receive international payments through Stripe.²

## Likely judge questions

- **"Why won't people just use Fiverr?"** Fiverr's fees, payout barriers and cold-start problem keep beginners out. We work with jobs from any source, including Facebook, from day one, and payment lands in a wallet Nepalis already use.
- **"Can't a client and worker fake a passport entry?"** The client must confirm it, the worker must confirm the payment, and the proof code makes edits visible. For a pilot, we'd add a light human check on each worker's first entry.
- **"Is the Work Passport an official ID?"** No. It's a portfolio and work record, and the app says so on the verify page.
- **"What about the 1 in 9 unpaid figure?"** It's reported by Samriddhi Foundation (≈11.1% of freelancers not receiving contracted payments).³ Quote it as "reported", not as our own data.
