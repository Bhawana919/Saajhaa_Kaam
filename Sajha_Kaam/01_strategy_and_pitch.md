# Sajha Kaam: the opportunity to stay and work globally

## 1. Decision on the “Sovereign Digital Sandbox”

**Verdict:** compelling as an opening thought experiment, weak as the product a team promises to build. I would not pitch “charter digital zones” or international legal identity as an achieved hackathon solution. Nepal's investment and foreign exchange regimes are national, and a municipal pilot cannot silently replace employment law, tax obligations or the applicable law of a foreign buyer. The word *sovereign* invites a judge to ask who granted sovereignty. [Department of Industry](https://www.doind.gov.np/foreign-investment); [Nepal Rastra Bank](https://www.nrb.org.np/departments/fxm/).

**What survives:** standardized agreements, verifiable deliverables, transparent payments, a human dispute path, and locally accessible support. These create a practical *trust layer* without inventing a jurisdiction. Call a university or municipality partnership a **skills and access pilot**, not a charter zone. Any legal clauses are configurable templates reviewed for specific use, not Singaporean or Delaware law automatically applied in Nepal.

**Hackathon potential:** high if the team shows a real end-to-end transaction with a client and a worker, a believable payout arrangement, and evidence of demand. Moderate if all the team shows is UI and speculative partnerships. A shortlist or win cannot be guaranteed. Judge against likely criteria: problem specificity; credible novelty; working demo; route to adoption; measurable social impact; legal feasibility.

| Original claim | Demo-safe replacement |
|---|---|
| “International legal identity” | Consent-based verified profile and exportable work record; no official status |
| “Singapore/Delaware law governs the worker” | Jurisdiction-specific reviewed contract choices; disclose the real governing law |
| “Municipality creates a digital jurisdiction” | Municipality/university recruits trainees and offers access and mentoring |
| “Platform escrow protects everyone” | Milestone approval and receipt tracking; licensed partner handles money if contracted |
| “Diaspora invests in workers” | Diaspora first purchases services; regulated investments only as a separate future product |

## 2. Three lenses

**Philosopher — agency and dignity.** A good platform expands the worker's choices. It does not imply migration is a failure or promise everyone can stay. It gives workers control over visibility, pricing, evidence, data portability and the option to leave the platform. Success is a decent paid choice, not a daily streak.

**Policy maker — complement institutions.** Pilot through a public skills center or university, while tax, foreign exchange, labor classification and investor protections remain subject to actual national and destination-country rules. Publish aggregate outcomes with consent; avoid making government sponsorship a prerequisite for participation. The platform should be auditable without being a new regulator.

**Big tech product team — solve the liquidity and trust loop.** A generic jobs marketplace starts empty on both sides. Start with a narrow buyer segment: diaspora-run SMEs purchasing bounded digital tasks in English and Nepali, such as product catalog cleanup, short video editing and customer support setup. Source five real buyers before recruiting 100 workers. Paid one-week pilots, clear deliverables, dispute rules and work samples shorten the path to a first verified transaction. Expand categories only when repeat purchase works.

## 3. One chosen product

**Name:** Sajha Kaam (साझा काम), “shared work.” Tagline: **Local talent. Global work. Clear terms.** Confirm naming and trademark availability before launch.

**Users:** (1) Nepali talent aged 18+ who can perform remotely deliverable-based work; (2) diaspora-run SMEs and global clients who can specify a small paid task; (3) an operations reviewer; (4) optional mentors, with no right to view private project data unless invited.

**Core loop:** client posts a fixed-scope paid pilot → worker applies with one relevant sample → client selects worker → both accept milestone, price, deadline and rights → worker delivers → client accepts or requests one revision → licensed partner reports settlement → verified outcome is added to a portable Work Passport. Show clear failure and dispute states as part of the loop.

**Differentiation:** the *first paid proof* is the product. A CV alone is weak; a paid, bounded, reproducible result with client confirmation helps a newcomer compete. Workers own a downloadable portfolio link and can revoke share access. Platform transaction evidence remains auditable and is labeled by source.

**No diaspora investment feature in the MVP.** “Capital” here means a client purchasing a service. A future diaspora capital module could introduce applicants to regulated financial providers with explicit eligibility and approvals. It cannot offer securities, pooled wallets, loans, promised returns or crowdfunding just because an app calls it community support.

## 4. Does gamification belong here?

**Use a small amount of progression, tied to capability:** three transparent steps, “Create a sample → finish a paid pilot → earn a repeat client.” Each completed step offers a useful next action, with a certificate only when the evidence warrants it. Provide optional mentor feedback and a private progress checklist.

**Avoid points, public leaderboards, fake streaks and bid races.** They reward screen time or underpricing, create incentives to fabricate outcomes and can make economic stress feel like a game. Do not rank access to work by how much a worker pays or by a reputation score that cannot be challenged. Label *completed work*, *client-confirmed work* and *partner-confirmed settlement* separately; do not combine them into one misleading “verified” badge.

**Experiment:** randomize access to the private three-step checklist versus a plain onboarding checklist, never visibility or pay. Predefine a 30-day metric: fraction completing a quality-reviewed sample and submitting one qualified application. Monitor complaints, task completion quality and differences by gender, language, disability and low-bandwidth devices. Stop the feature if it increases applications but harms completion or well-being.

## 5. Hackathon demo and test plan

**In the demo:** a clickable mobile web app with talent/client modes, short project listing, proof-backed application, milestone agreement, file delivery, client acceptance, simulated settlement update, exportable Work Passport and dispute initiation. Show a small operator review screen. A video of a user completing the flow can accompany the prototype.

**60-second live path:** worker discovers a product catalog task; submits a portfolio sample; client hires for a $60 one-week pilot; both see a plain-language milestone and revision limit; worker submits; client accepts; transaction moves to “payout pending” in the demo; worker exports proof. Dispute path shown as a second branch. $60 and all names are illustrative.

**Four-week pilot proposal:** recruit 20 vetted workers and five diaspora-led companies through a university/skills center and community networks. Secure one bank or licensed provider for a documented settlement arrangement before processing real money. First two weeks: usability and matching interviews. Next two: 10 small paid pilot opportunities, support and dispute review. Targets, not historical results: at least five paid completed projects; median time to first qualified shortlist under 7 days; 90% milestones with clear scope and price; no unresolved overdue payouts beyond partner SLA. Track worker net earnings after fees and client repeat intent. Do not claim to reduce emigration from a four-week pilot.

**Business model hypothesis:** client service fee disclosed before posting, for example 5% subject to buyer interviews and actual payment costs; no worker application fee. The exact fee is a test variable, not a validated price. The platform never charges for a badge.

**Top risks and mitigations:**

| Risk | First response |
|---|---|
| No buyer demand | Sign purchase intent for bounded tasks before expanding talent supply |
| Unpaid or late work | Milestone acceptance, reminders, written evidence, support, and partner settlement status |
| Worker misclassification | Restrict first pilot to project services; legal review for recurring/supervised arrangements |
| Fraud or fabricated proof | Human check for first transaction; source labels; reversible trust flags and appeals |
| Off-platform leakage | Good client value, portable proofs and clear terms; do not lock users in |
| Regional/digital exclusion | Nepali/English, low-data mode, assisted enrollment and offline workshops |
| Payment/compliance mismatch | No wallet or custody; licensed partner and legal review before payment activation |

## 6. Two-minute pitch distilled

“Nepal doesn't lack talent; it lacks a short, trustworthy route from talent to a first global customer. Sajha Kaam helps a client buy one clearly scoped piece of work, protects both sides with visible milestones and a human resolution path, and lets a worker carry proof of real results. We can pilot it through existing skills hubs and regulated payment partners. One paid project is modest, but repeatable paid projects create a real choice.”

## 7. Evidence and caveat

The ILO reports over **740,000 labour permits** for FY 2023/24. This is not a count of unique emigrants; the brief's 200,000 figure should not be used without a cited definition. The World Bank identifies limited quality domestic jobs and remittances of about one quarter of GDP in 2023. These sources motivate the project, but neither proves the platform will prevent migration. Full links and interpretation are in `sources.md`.
